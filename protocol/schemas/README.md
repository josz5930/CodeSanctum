# Protocol Schemas

Home for JSON Schema 2020-12 artifact contracts.

Story 1.3 adds the first real `codeattest.v0` schemas. Stable schema ids use `urn:codeattest:protocol:v0:<schema-name>` until there is a reviewed public schema-hosting location.

Required v0 schemas cover review scope, scanner finding sets, disclosure policy, outbound manifest, customer approval, bundle manifest, local runner attempts, environment evidence gates, artifact reference, retention/source-derived class, identity signing input, and signature envelope. `shared-definitions.schema.json` holds common definitions such as `protocol_version`, UTC timestamps, coverage modes, actor references, and algorithm-prefixed SHA-256 ids.

`scanner-finding-set.schema.json` is the Candidate Finding contract for Local Runner scanner adapters. It records scanner execution metadata, file-level scanned coverage, candidate-only findings, optional local raw-output artifact references, and coverage limitations; it must not allow reviewer classifications or expert-review states.

`disclosure-policy.schema.json` records per-review evidence disclosure intent before any outbound manifest or bundle is prepared. Story 1.6 limits `coverage_mode` to exactly three v1 values: `metadata_only`, `finding_context_snippets`, and `extended_approved_snippets_or_targeted_files`. The policy records review-scope and scanner-finding-set references, explicit evidence categories, snippet caps without snippet content, redaction metadata, source-derived retention classes, mode-specific warnings, and limitations. Raw Snippets and targeted files default to `transient_source_derived`; retained source requires explicit customer opt-in plus a retention period.

`outbound-manifest.schema.json` is the Story 1.7 local approval surface. It records the manifest identity, selected review-scope summary, runner metadata, Disclosure Policy summary, Coverage Mode, complete evidence category rows, local artifact references, package preview state, warnings, and limitations before any send action exists. `evidence_categories` must explicitly represent `metadata`, `dependencies`, `scanner_findings`, `raw_snippets`, `targeted_files`, `derived_artifacts`, and `never_collected_items`; sparse manifests are not valid. Each row carries inclusion state, count/reference, source-derived class, source-code disclosure label, redaction state/configuration version, retention handling, limitation, and display details so terminal and UI renderers do not invent category semantics. `package_preview_state` is preview-only and not send-ready, and `approval.approval_state` remains `not_requested` for Story 1.7.

Story 1.8 keeps outbound manifests preview-safe and adds separate `customer-approval.schema.json` and strengthened `bundle-manifest.schema.json` contracts. Customer approval records the displayed manifest context, decision, timestamp, optional actor reference, acknowledged warnings, and declined `not_submitted` next actions. Bundle manifests require an approved customer approval reference, preserve the approved `manifest_id`, remain `not_submitted`, include review-scope / Disclosure Policy / outbound-manifest / approval artifact references, expose verification metadata, and record local cleanup intent for transient source-derived artifacts. They do not create Vendor Receipt, received state, Attestation, or submission semantics.

Story 1.9 adds `local-runner-attempt.schema.json` for local attempt and status records. A record captures the failed or inspected stage, outcome, review/approval/bundle state, `remote_state: not_submitted`, runner version, runner trust metadata, stable local identities when available, privacy-safe diagnostics, and next actions. Failed pre-approval records cannot carry bundle identities. Post-approval failures must preserve `manifest_id`, `approval_id`, decision metadata, and the no-signed-bundle-ready state. Ready local bundles may carry `evidence_bundle_id`, `bundle_instance_id`, and `submission_attempt_id`, but still remain not submitted.


`signature-envelope.schema.json` records typed signatures over canonical identities and metadata, not archive bytes. It admits exactly one algorithm profile (`ml_dsa_65`) and two signing modes (`managed_key`, `enrolled_runner_key`); `signature_bytes` must be a real 3309-byte ML-DSA-65 signature. Explicit `signing_limitations` still state whose custody the key is under and what a valid signature does not attest to.

Story 2.0 hardens Epic 2 trust prerequisites. `artifact-reference.schema.json` now treats `content_path` as a portable protocol path rather than a host-local filesystem path: paths must be relative, slash-separated, non-traversing, and paired with `content_path_anchor` so manifest and bundle identities do not encode machine-local roots. `environment-evidence-gate.schema.json` distinguishes `synthetic_demo`, `partner_pilot_candidate`, and `partner_pilot_real_snippet_ready` profiles and makes real Raw Snippet / targeted-file acceptance depend on explicit access-control, access-logging, encryption, retention, deletion, budget, and signing/release-trust readiness fields.

Sub-project G Task 1 adds `environment-readiness-evidence.schema.json` and `environment-readiness-decision.schema.json`. Evidence records are content-addressed, pilot/release/deployment-bound, attachment-digest-only records with explicit producer/reviewer separation and passed/failed results. Decisions are content-addressed by excluding their id and signature, bind exactly one record for each readiness control, record distinct security/operations approvers, and require an ML-DSA-65 managed-key signature when approved. A final `environment-evidence-gate` must carry `readiness_decision_ref`; the referenced approved decision hashes the canonical gate approval input with only that reference omitted, avoiding circular identity.

Story 2.3 adds `vendor-receipt.schema.json` as the protocol-owned receipt contract. A Vendor Receipt is valid only for `received_with_receipt`, binds the approved manifest and received bundle identities, selected commit, repository identity hash, Coverage Mode, Disclosure Policy summary, explicit approved/received artifact count summaries, and public verification metadata. Receipt signatures reuse `signature-envelope.schema.json` with `signed_identity_type: "vendor_receipt"`; signing-input fixtures use `signing_input_type: "vendor_receipt_identity"`.

Keep protocol-owned object fields `snake_case` and closed with `additionalProperties: false`. If future schemas need map-like extension fields, constrain those maps explicitly and add shared fixtures before adapters depend on them.

Story 2.6 adds `submission-outcome.schema.json` as the protocol artifact for any intake result: `outcome_state` is exactly one of `received_with_receipt`, `rejected_no_receipt`, or `quarantined_no_receipt`, a receipted success carries `vendor_receipt_ref` and no `failure_reason_codes`, and both failure states carry at least one reason code and no receipt reference. `failure_reason_codes` is a `pattern`-constrained snake_case vocabulary rather than an enum, because the codes are owned by `services/intake` and must stay free to grow without a protocol version bump. Failed and quarantined submissions are recorded in the existing `review-event-log` as `submission_rejected` / `submission_quarantined` events that reference the outcome artifact and derive idempotency from `(bundle_instance_id, submission_attempt_id)`. No second attempt-history schema is introduced; quarantine storage, release, expiry, and operator workflow remain out of scope.

`log-checkpoint.schema.json` is the protocol-owned transparency projection over the append-only review-event logs. Existing events remain unchanged: their content-addressed `event_id` values and `sequence_number` positions produce deterministic per-review chain heads, and a deployment-scoped Merkle root commits the sorted set of those heads. Each checkpoint is RFC 8785 content-addressed through `checkpoint_id`, records `demo` or `pilot` explicitly, and is unsigned at this layer. Host exposure, signing, scheduling, and off-box publication belong to the later F-9 delivery work.

Story 3.1 adds `review-finding-draft-set.schema.json` as the protocol-owned normalization output for received scanner Candidate Findings. It consumes `scanner-finding-set.schema.json` as upstream input and emits Review Finding drafts with candidate provenance, grouping, affected area, evidence references, scanner-provided severity/confidence, scanner rule ids, Coverage Mode, evidence basis, threshold gaps, source-reference state, text-first draft lifecycle state, and retention/source-derived class. The artifact is receipt-gated by a `vendor_receipt_ref` plus evidence bundle and manifest identities, but it does not mint receipts, execute scanners, create expert classifications, add remediation guidance, generate validation paths, or write Attestation copy. No-findings normalization uses the claim-safe statement "No findings were produced by the configured inputs" and explicitly states that this does not prove absence of vulnerabilities.

Story 3.2 adds `finding-classification-record.schema.json` as the protocol-owned expert judgment artifact. It references a Story 3.1 Review Finding draft, carries the draft evidence refs that the reviewer saw, limits reviewer taxonomy to `likely`, `confirmed`, `inconclusive`, and `requires_customer_side_validation`, records evidence basis, confirmation criteria, threshold gaps, limitations, rationale, source-reference state, retained review artifact class, and visibility. `confirmed` requires meaningful confirmation criteria, and any scanner/metadata/unavailable basis also requires defensible threshold criteria even when submitted evidence is present. Semantic gates bind `evidence_basis` to `review_finding_draft_evidence_refs` and reject source-reference contradictions. `requires_customer_side_validation` requires a meaningful validation-path summary or claim-safe reference. Remediation implementation, customer owner/status, accepted-risk or false-positive records, Attestation copy, scanner execution fields, and raw snippet/source content do not belong in this record.

Story 3.3 adds `finding-remediation-guidance.schema.json`, `customer-remediation-status-record.schema.json`, and `customer-facing-finding-record.schema.json` as separate protocol-owned retained review artifacts. Reviewer remediation guidance references a classification record and Review Finding draft evidence, uses the bounded guidance statuses `actionable_guidance_provided`, `limited_guidance_requires_validation`, and `guidance_unavailable_from_submitted_evidence`, and keeps conditional business rules in semantic validators rather than schema composition. Customer remediation status records capture customer owner/status/due-date/target-state/notes and explicit export posture as audit metadata; they do not rewrite expert classification or reviewer rationale and do not introduce accepted-risk or false-positive states. Customer-facing finding records are derived read models that keep expert classification, evidence basis/limitations, reviewer remediation guidance, customer remediation status metadata, verification state, and later accepted-risk/false-positive visibility separate. Story 3.3 also adds `remediation_guidance_recorded` to review-event schemas for reviewer-authored guidance while preserving `customer_remediation_recorded` for customer-authored status updates.

Story 3.4 adds `finding-validation-path.schema.json` and `reviewer-validation-script.schema.json` as formal validation artifacts that replace the earlier lightweight validation-path handoff ambiguity. Validation paths are reviewer-authored retained review artifacts tied to a classification record and Review Finding draft; they keep remote dynamic testing, customer-run scripts, and manual steps distinct, and record required evidence, steps, expected result, limitations, and included-pass verifiability without claiming verification complete. Reviewer validation scripts are approved Review Artifact content with purpose, prerequisites, execution steps, expected output, safety notes, attachment instructions, and either an included base-package slot 1..3 or `additional_script_candidate_pricing_tbd` posture. Formal script bodies do not belong in classification, remediation guidance, customer status, or Review Finding draft schemas.

Story 3.5 adds `false-positive-record.schema.json` and `accepted-risk-record.schema.json` as separate outcome artifacts. False positives are reviewer-authored retained review artifacts with rationale, evidence basis, limitations, actor, timestamp, and a normalized Review Finding draft/classification reference; they do not delete or hide the finding. Accepted risk records preserve customer-backed rationale or sign-off evidence plus actor/timestamp, limitations, optional scope/owner/review-by metadata, and classification/finding references. Accepted risk and false positive are not values in `finding-classification-record.classification`, not customer remediation statuses, and not verification/Attestation claims. `customer-facing-finding-record.schema.json` now supports record-backed `accepted_risk_outcome` and `false_positive_outcome` sections while preserving the earlier top-level refs and `future_outcome_visibility` compatibility fields.

Story 4.1 adds `verification-pass-scope.schema.json` as the protocol-owned selection/scope artifact for the included verification pass. It records customer-selected Review Finding draft refs, current classification/remediation/status context, requested verification type, eligibility state, the 30-day included-pass deadline, included Validation Script slot allocation 1..3, additional-script candidates with pricing TBD posture, customer-backed selection provenance, limitations, retained review artifact class, and visibility. It is selection/scope only: follow-up commits, uploaded validation evidence, before/after verification decisions, and addendum projection remain separate artifacts.

Story 4.2 adds `verification-evidence-record.schema.json`, a closed metadata-only record for follow-up commits or validation artifacts. It binds the active scope version, selected finding/classification, original/follow-up commit and repository identities or formal validation path/script, integrity metadata, tenant/review access scope, environment profile, retention class, actor/customer backing, non-final intake state, limitations, and next step. Payload/content fields and verification outcomes are not part of this schema.

Story 4.3 adds `verification-record.schema.json`, a reviewer-owned before/after decision. It preserves the original classification, draft evidence references, evidence basis, source-reference state, and recorded confirmation criteria; binds one or more verification evidence records; records per-criterion after-state results, rationale, limitations, and canonical outcome; and requires adapters to keep incomplete states actionable. Legacy labels are compatibility inputs only and are not protocol output.

Story 4.4 adds `verification-addendum.schema.json`, a standalone customer-facing projection over original review scope, selected commit/repository identity, verification scope, per-finding reviewer decisions/evidence refs, retained evidence, deletion evidence/status, history refs, limitations, and finalization. It is not an Attestation, signature envelope, certification artifact, or new review authority.

Epic 5 adds protocol-owned `security-review-attestation`, `supporting-evidence-mapping`, `static-bundle-manifest`, `static-portal-projection`, `attestation-package-finalization`, `pilot-metric-record`, and `pilot-feedback-record` contracts. The Attestation is a versioned, customer-safe retained projection with independently readable scope, method, receipt, findings/classification, remediation/validation, verification, lifecycle, and limitation sections. Optional mappings are approved and versioned, name evidence-consumer decision authority, and describe supporting evidence only; the v0 vocabulary permits SOC 2 supporting-evidence, generic technology-risk, and customer-security-review profiles, not regional claims or acceptance conclusions.

`static-bundle-manifest.schema.json` keeps `static_bundle_id` stable while each `static_bundle_manifest_id` identifies one canonical manifest version. It covers every included file digest, the verifiable Evidence Bundle representation, Vendor Receipt and Attestation refs, minimization disposition, portal projection, and offline verification metadata. Finalization is customer-owned and records a newly signed higher manifest version; generated, finalized, and exported events are distinct. `static-portal-projection.schema.json` permits only self-contained relative assets with fixed navigation and offline search/print/copy capability. Pilot metric and feedback records are content-free, PII-free where applicable, and `internal_only`; they may never enter Attestation, bundle, portal, search/print, or customer event projections.
